# Relay Integrated Brand Experience Design Skill (`relay-brand-experience`)

## Purpose

This is an Integrated Brand Experience Design Skill.

It transforms a business idea, product, service, or brand into a coherent digital experience by combining four disciplines that are normally treated separately:

1. **MARKETING**
2. **HUMAN PSYCHOLOGY**
3. **VISUAL / BRAND IDENTITY**
4. **WEB / INTERACTION DESIGN**

Do not treat these as four independent tasks. Treat them as one connected system.

The objective is not merely to make a website look good. The objective is to create a website where:

- the brand has a clear identity
- the customer understands the value
- the customer feels something
- the interface behaves like the brand
- the visual identity reinforces the positioning
- the psychology supports the intended behavior
- the marketing message is embedded into the experience
- the website differentiates the company
- the design creates memory
- the experience supports conversion
- every major creative decision has a reason

## The Central Philosophy

A normal workflow:

```
Business → copy → website → logo → marketing
```

This skill uses:

```
Business → human insight → strategic positioning → desired perception → creative concept → identity → experience → behavior → marketing
```

The website is not merely where marketing happens. The website itself becomes part of the marketing, the brand, and the psychological experience.

The logo is not merely an icon. The logo becomes a compressed visual expression of the brand concept.

The interaction is not merely animation. The interaction becomes part of the brand's personality.

The page is not merely a collection of sections. The page becomes a journey.

**Goal: "Make the visitor experience the idea."**

### Core Principle Chain

```
BRAND → HUMAN INSIGHT → MARKETING STRATEGY → PSYCHOLOGY → CREATIVE CONCEPT → VISUAL IDENTITY → LOGO/SYMBOL → WEB EXPERIENCE → INTERACTION → EMOTION → MEMORY → BEHAVIOR → CONVERSION → BUSINESS OUTCOME
```

## THIS IS A SKILL, NOT A GENERIC WEBSITE GENERATOR

Do not immediately start designing a website.

First understand the business and the human problem.

Then determine:

- what the brand should mean
- what the customer should feel

- what the customer should want

- what the brand should be remembered for

Only then translate those conclusions into design.

## INPUT CONTRACT

When a new project begins, ask ONLY the minimum number of questions required to establish the strategic foundation. Do not interrogate the user with dozens of questions. Ask approximately 8–12 high-value questions. Prefer questions that unlock multiple downstream decisions.

### 1. WHAT IS IT?
What is the product, service, company, or idea?

### 2. WHO IS IT FOR?
Who is the primary customer?

### 3. WHAT PROBLEM DOES IT SOLVE?
What problem or desire does it address?

### 4. WHAT DOES THE CUSTOMER WANT TO FEEL?
If they choose this brand, what should they feel? (confident, sophisticated, free, safe, excited, powerful, calm, creative, understood, exclusive — do not force these examples)

### 5. WHAT SHOULD THE CUSTOMER BELIEVE?
After interacting with the brand, what should they believe about the product, the company, themselves, and the future state they can achieve?

### 6. WHAT IS THE DESIRED ACTION?
What should the website ultimately make the visitor do? (buy, book, subscribe, sign up, contact, request a demo, explore, trust, remember)

### 7. WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT?
What is genuinely different from competitors?

### 8. WHAT SHOULD THE BRAND FEEL LIKE?
Ask for adjectives, references, brands, environments, objects, places, films, products, or experiences that capture the desired feeling.

### 9. WHAT MUST WE AVOID?
Ask for styles, clichés, competitors, visual directions, tones, or behaviors the user explicitly does not want.

### 10. WHAT ARE THE PRACTICAL CONSTRAINTS?
Ask only when relevant: budget, timeline, technology, mobile-first requirement, accessibility, performance, content availability, existing brand assets.

**If the user already supplied information, DO NOT ask again. Infer what can safely be inferred. Clearly label assumptions.**

Determine the psychological system behind the experience.

Research / reason about relevant mechanisms such as:

attention, curiosity, surprise, novelty, expectation violation, social proof, identity, status, belonging, aspiration, anticipated emotion, mental simulation, self-congruity, psychological ownership, loss aversion, scarcity, authority, trust, cognitive fluency, processing fluency, memory, narrative transportation, discovery, agency, immersion, presence, reward, reciprocity, commitment, behavioral friction, choice architecture

**DO NOT force psychological concepts into the design. Only use mechanisms that are relevant to the actual business and audience.**

For each important psychological mechanism answer:

- What is it?
- Why is it relevant?
- What behavior could it influence?
- How should it appear in the experience?
- What evidence supports it?
- What are its risks?

## STAGE 3 — FUTURE-STATE / EXPERIENCE DESIGN

Ask: "What should it FEEL LIKE to use / own / experience this product?"

Do not stop at product features. Translate the product into an experiential state.

Examples (derive actual qualities from the brand, do not use these as generic prescriptions):

- A sauna → warmth → humidity → condensation → privacy → slowness → calm → physical relief
- A luxury product → craftsmanship → status → restraint → exclusivity
- A performance product → speed → precision → energy → control
- A financial product → clarity → security → control → confidence
- A creative studio → experimentation → imagination → intelligence → craft

Then ask: "Can the website itself reproduce some of this feeling?"

This is a core principle of the skill.

## STAGE 18 — DESIGN VALIDATION

Before declaring the concept successful, run these 12 tests:

1. **BRAND TEST** — Does the experience feel like the brand?
2. **PSYCHOLOGY TEST** — Is there a defensible reason for the psychological mechanism?
3. **MARKETING TEST** — Does the experience communicate the desired value and positioning?
4. **CREATIVITY TEST** — Is the idea genuinely different, or merely visually fashionable?
5. **STORY TEST** — Is there a coherent narrative?
6. **EXPERIENCE TEST** — Does the user FEEL something?
7. **MEMORY TEST** — What will the visitor remember tomorrow?
8. **CONVERSION TEST** — Does the creativity support the business objective?
9. **USABILITY TEST** — Can people actually use it?
10. **ACCESSIBILITY TEST** — Can people with different abilities experience it?
11. **PERFORMANCE TEST** — Does the experience remain fast and responsive?
12. **"COULD ANYONE DO THIS?" TEST** — Could a competitor copy the same idea without changing the meaning? If yes, investigate whether the concept is sufficiently brand-specific.

## STAGE 23 — HIGH-AESTHETIC CODE EXECUTION MANDATE

Whenever generating actual code, HTML/CSS/JS, React components, or visual prototypes for the website, strictly enforce these 8 visual craftsmanship rules to guarantee an Awwwards / Apple / Vercel grade finish:

1. **NO RAW DEFAULT COLORS**: Never use basic `#ffffff`, `#000000`, `blue`, `red`, `purple`. Always define and use custom HSL CSS tokens (`--bg: hsl(240, 15%, 4%)`, `--surface: hsl(240, 12%, 8%)`, `--accent: hsl(217, 91%, 60%)`, `--border: rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.08)`).
2. **MODERN TYPOGRAPHY SYSTEM**: Always import Google Fonts (`Outfit`, `Syne`, `Cabinet Grotesk`, `Plus Jakarta Sans`, `Inter`, `Space Grotesk`, `Cormorant Garamond`). Enforce negative tracking on display headings (`letter-spacing: -0.03em`), proportional line heights (`1.1` to `1.25` for display, `1.6` for body text), and uppercase monospaced micro-labels (`11px font-mono tracking-widest text-muted`).
3. **GLASSMORPHISM & SPECULAR LIGHTING**: Combine `backdrop-filter: blur(20px)`, low-opacity background gradients (`linear-gradient(180deg, rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.05) 0%, rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.01) 100%)`), and 1px specular top/side borders (`border-top: 1px solid rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.15)`).
4. **AMBIENT ATMOSPHERICS & RADIAL GLOWS**: Use background radial ambient glows (`background: radial-gradient(circle at 50% 0%, rgba(0, 114, 245, 0.15), transparent 70%)`) and multi-layered soft shadows (`box-shadow: 0 20px 40px -15px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.5)`).
5. **BENTO GRID ARCHITECTURE**: Structure features using asymmetrical modular grid layouts (`display: grid; grid-template-columns: repeat(12, 1fr)`), high visual loudness contrast (scale hierarchy), and 24px container border radius.
6. **LUXURY SPACING**: Maintain generous vertical whitespace (`padding: 120px 24px` for hero and major sections, `gap: 24px` for grid items). Never crowd content against container borders.
7. **MICRO-INTERACTION PHYSICS**: Apply spring/cubic-bezier motion (`transition: all 0.3s cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`), hover transforms (`transform: translateY(-4px)`), active button state feedback, and subtle hover glow highlights.
8. **ZERO BROWSER DEFAULTS**: Fully style custom scrollbars (`::-webkit-scrollbar`), zero out default button/input borders, add custom badge styling, hover reveals, and zero unstyled text links.

## CRITICAL RULES

### RULE 1: DO NOT OVER-DESIGN

The purpose of this skill is NOT to make every website experimental, animated, 3D, interactive, luxurious, minimal, futuristic, or artistic. The correct experience depends on the brand.

A simple website can be extraordinary if the concept, psychology, content, identity, and execution are exceptionally coherent.

A technically spectacular website can be terrible if it has no strategic reason to exist.

**DO NOT ADD COMPLEXITY TO LOOK CREATIVE. ADD COMPLEXITY ONLY WHEN IT CREATES MEANING, EXPERIENCE, OR BUSINESS VALUE.**

### RULE 2: PSYCHOLOGY MUST NOT BECOME MANIPULATION

Use psychology to:
- improve understanding
- reduce uncertainty
- create appropriate emotion
- communicate value
- create memorable experiences
- help people make informed decisions

Do not use:
- deception
- fake scarcity
- fake social proof
- dark patterns
- coercive interaction
- hidden cancellation
- intentionally confusing interfaces

**Goal: PERSUASION THROUGH BETTER EXPERIENCE, not manipulation.**

### RULE 3: MARKETING MUST NOT DESTROY THE EXPERIENCE

Do not turn every section into a sales pitch. The website should balance:
- information
- emotion
- trust
- storytelling
- exploration
- persuasion
- conversion

Marketing should be embedded in the experience rather than pasted onto it.

### RULE 4: LOGO IS NOT A SEPARATE UNIVERSE

Logo design is part of the larger identity system. Use logo research when it strengthens brand story, recognition, symbolism, differentiation, and visual language.

Do not force a dual-story logo merely because the technique is clever.

A logo is successful when: **MEANING + RECOGNITION + RELEVANCE + APPLICATION** work together.

### RULE 5: CREATIVE ≠ RANDOM

A design is not creative merely because it is unusual. Evaluate:

**NOVELTY + MEANING + RELEVANCE + PSYCHOLOGICAL EFFECT + EXECUTION**

The best ideas create: "I have never seen this before." followed by: "Now that I see it, it makes perfect sense."

### RULE 6: THE WEBSITE SHOULD HAVE ONE CENTRAL IDEA

Do not create one visual idea for the hero, another unrelated animation for section two, another trend for section three, and another style for the footer.

Instead create **ONE CENTRAL EXPERIENCE CONCEPT** and allow logo + typography + layout + color + imagery + motion + interaction + copy to express that concept in different ways.

The website should feel like **ONE world**.

## FINAL DECISION FILTER

Before approving any major design decision, ask:

1. Does this strengthen the brand?
2. Does this help the user?
3. Does this communicate something?
4. Does this create the intended psychological response?
5. Does this support the marketing objective?
6. Does this fit the creative concept?
7. Is it memorable?
8. Is it usable?
9. Is it accessible?
10. Is it performant?
11. Is it necessary?
12. Could it be simpler while preserving the same effect?

If the answer to most is no, reject the decision.

---

## THE ULTIMATE PRINCIPLE

Do not design a logo, a website, an animation, a marketing campaign, a landing page, a color palette, or a typography system separately.

Treat all of them as manifestations of one thing: **THE BRAND EXPERIENCE.**

The final goal:

A person arrives → They immediately understand something → Then they feel something → Then they become curious → Then they explore → Then they discover something → Then they understand the value → Then they imagine themselves using / owning / experiencing the product → Then they trust the brand → Then they remember it → Then they act.

And when they leave, the person should be able to describe not only "What the company does" but **"How the company feels."**

That is the standard this skill must optimize for.

## A. Web Design Psychology

### The 50-Millisecond Window
Aesthetic judgments form within a 17–50ms neuro-cognitive window before conscious reading occurs. Core determinants:
- **Visual Complexity (VC)**: Cognitive effort to process/organize elements (governed by Gestalt principles: proximity, similarity, symmetry)
- **Prototypicality (PT)**: How closely the interface fits the user's pre-existing mental schema for that website category
- Users initially prefer **low VC** and **high PT**

### Tri-Level Emotional Processing (Donald Norman)
1. **Visceral**: Immediate, pre-conscious sensory reaction (attraction/repulsion)
2. **Behavioral**: Subconscious interaction fluidity, motor expectation fulfillment, low latency
3. **Reflective**: Conscious rationalization aligning the experience with personal identity, status, and memory

An extraordinary website achieves absolute congruence across all three levels.

### Contextual Inevitability
**The "Could Only This Brand Do It?" Test.** If a competitor could adopt a site's visual/interactive language without cognitive dissonance, the design is merely decorative.

### Predictive Processing & Processing Fluency
The brain functions as a prediction engine. Minimizing prediction errors by meeting architectural expectations creates **high processing fluency**, which the brain rewards with positive affect.

### Expectation Violation + Relative Fluency
To avoid forgettability, extraordinary design uses calculated violation of expectations to trigger an **"Aha!" moment**. The violation must be paired with **relative fluency** (an instantly comprehensible visual metaphor); unfocused violations merely create cognitive friction.

### Embodied Cognition & Digital Materiality
Human thought relies on physical body interactions. Coupling physical inputs (scroll, hover, drag) with responsive visual feedback builds tactile presence. Making pixels act like physical matter via WebGL, Canvas, and CSS filters creates **digital materiality** (e.g., CSS liquid glass using `feDisplacementMap` SVG primitive).

### Digital Atmospherics
Borrowing from environmental psychology (light, space, texture, acoustics) to manipulate visual density, whitespace, motion easing, and color temperature to alter time perception and dwell time.

### Digital Threshold
Interstitial entrance experiences (layered light, volumetric fog, low-opacity CTA, slow motion easing) that intentionally decelerate browsing velocity, lowering the user's cognitive state into deliberate attention.

### Narrative Transportation Theory & Scrollytelling
Deep narrative absorption causes users to mentally simulate stories, building emotional connections. **Scrollytelling** gives users agency over narrative pacing. Use **Pinned Storytelling** (`position: sticky` + `IntersectionObserver`) — never scroll-jacking.

|---|---|---|

| **Immersive Canvas** | Edge-to-edge 3D/imagery with floating UI | Luxury automotive, gaming |

## B. Marketing Psychology

### 7-Stage Genius Marketing Framework
1. **Strategic Deviation**: Violate established category schema to generate pattern interrupt
2. **Attention Capture**: Bypass neurological filters via unexpected inputs
3. **Emotional Arousal**: Trigger high-arousal emotions compelling action
4. **Identity Alignment**: Consumer evaluates stimulus against self-concept
5. **Conversation & Voluntary Sharing**: Aroused individuals share to serve social needs
6. **Cultural Relevance**: Mass sharing transforms brand into cultural reference
7. **Demand Generation**: Cultural momentum → lower CAC, elevated sales, pricing power

### Arousal Theory (Berger & Milkman)
- **High-arousal positive** (awe, humor) → Highly viral
- **High-arousal negative** (anger, anxiety, disgust) → Highly viral
- **Low-arousal** (sadness, contentment) → Suppresses sharing
- Virality driven by physiological arousal levels, not positive/negative valence

### Social Currency & Self-Enhancement
People share content to project expertise, status, humor, or community ties — never to help corporations. Engineer shareability through social currency.

### Brand Prominence Theory (Han, Nunes, Drèze)
| Quadrant | Wealth | Status Need | Preference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Patricians** | High | Low | Quiet, inconspicuous branding |
| **Parvenus** | High | High | Loud, conspicuous branding |
| **Poseurs** | Low | High | Loud counterfeit items |
| **Proletarians** | Low | Low | Indifferent to status signaling |

### Key Mechanisms
- **Loss Aversion / Prospect Theory** (Kahneman & Tversky): Losses inflict 2× the psychological pain of equivalent gains
- **Pattern Interrupt / Perceptual Contrast**: Brain ignores predictable stimuli, fixates on novel inputs
- **Reciprocity**: Two-sided incentive combining mutual benefit with loss aversion

### Distribution Patterns
| Pattern | Principle | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Parasitic Distribution** | Use existing networks as payload carriers | Implicit social proof |
| **Two-Sided Incentive** | Reward both referrer and referee | Reciprocity / altruism |
| **Product as PR** | Product traits force media coverage | Salience / absurdity |
| **Expectation Inversion** | Show exact opposite of category norms | Perceptual contrast |
| **Cultural Hijacking** | Insert brand into focal live cultural event | Authority bias / attention |

### Viral Coefficient
**k-factor > 1.0** = self-sustaining exponential growth

### 5-Step Diagnostic Matrix
Before any commercial initiative:
1. **Attention**: Does it create perceptual contrast or pattern interrupt?
2. **Emotion**: Does it trigger high-arousal emotion?
3. **Identity**: What does sharing allow consumers to communicate about themselves?
4. **Distribution**: Is there an asymmetric distribution engine?
5. **Business**: How does captured attention funnel into business metrics?

### Anti-Playbook (Debunked Myths)
1. Marketing should always evoke positive emotions → **Reality**: High-arousal negative emotions drive memory/virality
2. Manufactured exclusivity guarantees long-term brand equity → **Reality**: Exclusivity is acquisition, not retention
3. Expensive luxury requires loud branding → **Reality**: Elites pay for quiet branding
4. Paid ad spending scales linearly → **Reality**: Diminishing returns; need product-led distribution (k > 1.0)

## D. Creativity Science

### Bipartite Definition
Creativity requires both **Novelty** (originality, unexpectedness) and **Usefulness** (effectiveness, appropriateness)

### Four C Model
| Tier | Scope | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Mini-c** | Subjective internal insights | Student grasping a concept |
| **Little-c** | Everyday problem-solving | Repurposing household items |
| **Pro-c** | Professional domain expertise | Dual-meaning corporate logo |
| **Big-C** | Historic paradigm shifts | Einstein, Picasso |

### 3 Brain Networks for Creative Cognition
1. **Default Mode Network (DMN)**: Mind-wandering, associative generation, memory recombination
2. **Executive Control Network (ECN)**: Cognitive control, evaluation, refinement against constraints
3. **Salience Network (SN)**: Neural mediator switching between DMN and ECN

Highly creative individuals functionally couple DMN and ECN simultaneously.

### Semantic Distance & Analogical Reasoning
- Core mechanism bridging unrelated cognitive domains
- **Left Frontopolar Cortex**: Recruited during semantically distant analogical mapping
- Activation increases as semantic distance grows

### Schema Incongruity Theory (Mandler)
- Slight incongruity → ignored
- **Optimal incongruity** → active resolution → dopamine surge ("aha!")
- Extreme incongruity → frustration/blocking
- **Optimal Stimulation Level** (Berlyne): Medium incongruity motivates processing

### Peak Shift Principle (Ramachandran & Hirstein)
Organisms respond more intensely to exaggerated hyper-stimuli than to natural realistic ones. In design: isolating and amplifying defining characteristics while stripping redundant noise maximizes dopaminergic reward while lowering cognitive load.

### Benign Violation Theory (McGraw & Warren)
Humorous or clever design requires two simultaneous conditions:
1. **Normative Violation**: Threat to expectations, logic, social norms
2. **Benign Appraisal**: Perception that the violation is safe or harmless
Governed by **Psychological Distance** (temporal, spatial, social, hypothetical)

### Visual Rhetoric (McQuarrie & Mick)
- **Visual Schemes**: Excess regularity (symmetry, repetition) → processed automatically, aesthetic pleasure
- **Visual Tropes**: Irregularity (metaphor, pun, irony, substitution) → requires active resolution, increases cognitive elaboration, dwell time, brand recall

### Conceptual Blending Theory (Fauconnier & Turner)
`Input Space 1` + `Input Space 2` → map via `Generic Space` → `Blended Space` with emergent meaning
**Double-Scope Networks**: Blending inputs with entirely different organizing frames

### Servicescape Model (Bitner)
| Type | Design | Goal | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gaming** (Friedman) | Low ceilings, maze, no windows | Disorientation, sensory constraint | Unplanned action via external control |
| **Playground** (Thomas) | High ceilings, skylights, luxury art | Restoration, status elevation | Extended dwell time, perceived internal control |

### Brainstorming Debunked (Diehl & Stroebe)
**Nominal Groups** (individuals alone, then pool) > **Interacting Groups**

Three process losses:
1. **Production Blocking**: One speaker bottleneck
2. **Evaluation Apprehension**: Self-censorship from social fear
3. **Social Loafing**: Diffusion of responsibility

Optimal: Hybrid process (asynchronous individual generation → collective evaluation)

### Creative Constraints
Constraints force the mind off obvious heuristics into deep semantic searches. Experts risk **cognitive entrenchment**.

### O.P.C.E. Framework
1. **O — Observation**: Audit constraints, visual equity, audience schemas
2. **P — Psychology**: Conceptual blending, target Benign Violation sweet spot
3. **C — Context**: Environmental/narrative scaffolding (Restorative vs Focused servicescape)
4. **E — Execution**: Peak Shift simplification, visual rhetoric, ensure human intentionality

### Visual Equity
Accumulated psychological capital in visual identity. Redesign catastrophes (Tropicana: 20% sales drop; Gap: immediate retraction) prove that aesthetic modernization cannot supersede established cognitive schemas.

### Narrative Transportation (Green & Brock)
Deep immersion detaches from reality, suppresses counterarguing, bypasses analytical filters. **NAR Framework**: Narrate, Act, Resonate.

### STEPPS Virality (Berger)
High-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety) shared at exponentially higher rates than low-arousal emotions. Motion graphics and sound act as pre-cognitive arousal stimuli.

### Creativity Training
Meta-analysis effect size: **0.68** (moderately strong). Most effective programs use structured cognitive models: problem identification, constraint mapping, analogical reasoning, oscillating divergent/convergent thinking.
